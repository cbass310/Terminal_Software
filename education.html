1. Core Concepts

The Vig (Juice)

SPORTS

The built-in profit margin or "tax" that sportsbooks charge for taking a bet. It is the reason why standard point spreads are priced at -110 on both sides instead of +100. To be profitable long-term, a quantitative bettor must find edges that mathematically exceed the vig.

Implied Probability

MATH

The conversion of a sportsbook's odds into a percentage. It represents the probability of an outcome occurring as determined by the bookmaker. If Terminal Software calculates that an outcome has a 60% chance of happening, but the sportsbook's implied probability is only 52%, an edge exists.

2. Quantitative Betting

Expected Value (+EV)

CRITICAL

The holy grail of quantitative betting. Expected Value (+EV) is the mathematical advantage you hold over the sportsbook on a specific wager. A +EV bet means that if you placed the exact same wager infinite times, it would yield a net profit. Terminal Software scans the global market to find lines where retail books are lagging behind the sharpest institutional books, delivering these +EV anomalies directly to your dashboard.

Arbitrage (Arb)

STRATEGY

A risk-free trading strategy. Arbitrage occurs when two competing sportsbooks offer vastly different odds on the same event. By using the Terminal Software Arbitrage Calculator, you can place a carefully sized wager on both sides of the event at different books, guaranteeing a mathematical profit regardless of who wins the game.

Closing Line Value (CLV)

METRIC

The true measure of a profitable bettor. CLV compares the odds you wagered at versus the final odds offered by sharp sportsbooks right before the event begins. If you bet a team at +150 and they close at +120, you generated positive CLV. Consistent positive CLV guarantees long-term profitability, even amid short-term variance.

Kelly Criterion

MATH

A mathematical formula used to determine the optimal size of a series of bets. Instead of betting random amounts, the Kelly Criterion factors in your exact mathematical edge (+EV) and the sportsbook's odds to tell you precisely what percentage of your bankroll to wager in order to maximize compound growth while minimizing the risk of ruin.

3. Crypto Markets

Average Directional Index (ADX)

INDICATOR

The primary momentum engine used in the Terminal Software Crypto Radar. ADX does not indicate whether an asset is moving up or down; rather, it measures the sheer strength of the current trend. An ADX above 25 signifies a strong trend, while an ADX above 40 signifies extreme momentum, making it highly viable for breakout trading strategies.

Market Regime

ANALYSIS

The overarching state of an asset's market environment (e.g., Bullish Trending, Bearish Trending, Mean Reverting, or Ranging). Terminal Software algorithms analyze volatility and momentum to categorize assets into specific regimes, allowing traders to deploy the mathematically correct strategy for that specific environment rather than guessing.
